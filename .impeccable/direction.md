# HORIZON full redesign direction

The user's attached brief pins a replacement visual world: preserve the product's facts and behavior while replacing the existing composition language. Its direct instruction takes precedence over the concept seed. The visual world is an atlas of a working river-estuary field system, expressed differently in four environments: place-led documentary at Home, a scientific instrument at Observatory, an observation notebook in Report, and a compact operations console in Admin.

## Product mechanism and audience scene

HORIZON makes the sensing and telemetry path at Cồn Hô legible beside the observations it produces and their limits. A farmer or community member may read or submit a record outdoors on a phone; a researcher inspects evidence and provenance; an operator handles administrative work at a desk.

## Seven grounded visual systems considered

1. A Vietnamese agricultural season almanac: connects weather, crop timing, and local observations over time.
2. A Mekong Delta hydrographic survey sheet: ties channels, landmarks, coordinates, and measured positions to real geography.
3. A field notebook with dated entries: gives observations, evidence, and uncertainty a human record structure.
4. A scientific instrument readout: makes measurements and limits precise and scannable.
5. A documentary contact sheet: puts project-owned place, equipment, and people ahead of decorative brand art.
6. Equipment nameplates and service logs: make device identity, maintenance, and acknowledgement limits concrete.
7. A fold-out Cồn Hô field atlas: joins actual place imagery, a spatial drawing, station topology, technical annotations, and dated observations into one navigable system. The seed assigned candidate seven. The user's brief independently pins the same field-atlas/documentary/instrument/notebook direction.

## Direction contract

- **World:** an estuary field atlas. The actual island, project photography, equipment, station map, measured values, and records carry the identity. Paper-like surfaces belong to Home and Report; cool, clean instrument surfaces belong to Observatory; Admin is compact operational tooling. HORIZON's existing logo remains.
- **First viewport:** an unmistakable image of Cồn Hô and the project's own field context, with the location, HORIZON, its field-monitoring purpose, and the true 2-station/1-gateway system fact. No invented live value. The scene reads as a cover, not a marketing hero or dashboard.
- **Visitor path:** place → nodes and equipment → real data path → agricultural questions → current network state and its limits → dated field notes → community report and project contact.
- **Signature interaction:** select a real station in the observatory's geographic view and inspect its domain, source, freshness, and values together. When those values do not exist, show an explicit unavailable state. Retain existing report-step interactions and form validation.
- **Cross-surface reach:** reuse the field system's type hierarchy, identifiers, dividers, focus behavior, and restrained project colors. Compose each route for its own task; do not force identical cards or spacing.
- **Honest risk:** the current project record does not prove installed, calibrated, or currently reporting field hardware. The atlas must communicate that this is a real project system and distinguish project documentation from verified live telemetry.
- **User-pinned data grammar:** keep measured, external context, reference, device health, and data quality distinct; retain station/gateway identity; preserve explicit demo mode; do not turn stored records into device acknowledgements.
- **Responsive/motion:** design the scene and map for mobile first, preserve 44px targets and reduced-motion support, and use motion only to orient a reader or trace an existing data path.

## Seed challengers

All six dealt challengers were declined on audience identification and product clarity after fusion with HORIZON facts; none supplies enough reason to replace the pinned direction.

- **Seedbed lobes:** green capsule-led slabs conflict with the brief's request for documentary, spatial compositions and restrained branding. Keep only the discipline of making one visual region own each viewport.
- **Exploitation one-sheet:** competing title hierarchy obscures measurements and provenance. Keep its decisive scale contrast between one lead subject and supporting metadata.
- **Transforming silk cape:** the garment transformation has no meaningful relation to station topology. Keep its clear distinction between states only where it explains a real workflow.
- **Azulejo station hall:** ornamental tile artwork overstates a decorative brand system. Keep only its disciplined underlying grid for precise instrument alignment.
- **Drum-machine step row:** an audio sequencer's lighting and toggles do not fit the project's outdoor reading or observation tasks. Keep its clear sequencing for time-series labels where useful.
- **Hand-drawn zine:** mascots and hand-drawn annotations contradict the desired scientific/documentary credibility. Keep its concise explanatory annotations around real diagrams.
